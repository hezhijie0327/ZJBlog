## Section II · 阅读理解 Part A（40 分）

### Text 1（孤独感与大脑 · 21–25 答案 BACAD）

Recent research has found that social relationships are crucial, providing safety, resources, opportunities to have children, and so on. The fact that we find the feeling of loneliness so unpleasant often motivates us to reconnect with others, bringing with it all of these benefits. But it's not as simple as that. Feeling lonely can also induce social withdrawal and types of negative thinking, which can make it harder to connect with people. Studies have identified differences in brain areas associated with loneliness. In lonely young adults, areas of the brain related to social cognition and empathy have less dense white matter. But in lonely older adults, brain regions important for cognitive processing and emotional regulation are actually smaller in volume.

A recent study found that the brains of lonely people process the world idiosyncratically. The researchers asked participants to watch a series of video clips while inside an MRI scanner and found that non-lonely people showed very similar neural activity to each other, whereas lonely people showed brain activity that was dissimilar to each other and to the non-lonely participants. So lonely people appear to view the world differently to others.

This is also evident in how lonely people view fictional characters. Researchers in the US carried out brain scans on fans of the television series Game of Thrones while these fans decided whether various adjectives accurately described characters from the show. The authors of the study were able to identify activity in the brain that distinguished between real and fictional people. While the difference between these two categories was clear in non-lonely people, the boundary was blurred for lonelier people. These results suggest that feeling lonely may be associated with thinking of fictional characters in a way similar to real-world friends. However, given the design of the study, it's unclear whether the findings suggest loneliness causes this way of thinking or if considering fictional characters in this way causes people to feel lonely. And there's always the possibility that a third factor causes both outcomes.

If we look at these studies and what they seem to be telling us, loneliness is not only the perceived absence of others, but also a desire for connection. Whether that's thinking of fictional characters like real friends or being drawn to happy objects, our brains seem to search for social connections wherever they might find them, especially when we don't feel like other humans are providing enough of these for us.

**21.** According to the passage, the unpleasantness of loneliness can ______.

- [A] weaken social cognitive ability
- [B] drive people to seek social ties
- [C] reduce brain white matter density
- [D] improve emotional regulation

<details>
<summary>21 · 细节因果 · 同义替换链</summary>

**答案：B**

**定位**：P1——"the feeling of loneliness so unpleasant often **motivates** us to **reconnect** with others"。

**替换链**：motivates → **drive**；reconnect with others → **seek social ties**。原文和选项没有一个词重复，但意思严丝合缝——这就是正确项的长相。

**其他选项在装什么**：A、C、D 全是孤独对**大脑**的生理影响（白质密度、认知区、情绪调节）——它们偷换了主语：题目问的是"不适感**驱动人做什么**"，不是"孤独改变了什么"。

**takeaway**：细节因果题 = 定位到因果句 + 认出动词短语的同义改写。原词复现的选项反而要警惕。
</details>

**22.** What did brain studies reveal about lonely young adults?

- [A] Smaller volume in cognitive regions.
- [B] Denser white matter in empathy areas.
- [C] Less dense white matter in social areas.
- [D] Larger brain regions for emotion control.

<details>
<summary>22 · 细节对比 · 张冠李戴识别</summary>

**答案：C**

**定位**：P2——"In lonely **young** adults, areas of the brain related to social cognition and empathy have **less dense white matter**"。

**替换链**：less dense white matter in social areas → 几乎原词搬运 → C。

**其他选项在装什么**：A（体积更小）和 D（区域更大）是**老年组**（older adults）的特征——本段最大的陷阱就是 young/old 两组数据并列，考你有没有看清"谁是谁的"；B 把 less dense 反着说成 denser。

**takeaway**：对比型段落（年轻人 vs 老人）出细节题，八成考"数据归谁"——定位后第一件事核对**主语人群**。
</details>

**23.** The MRI study on video-watching showed that lonely people ______.

- [A] process the world uniquely
- [B] share similar neural patterns
- [C] react more strongly to clips
- [D] show no empathy for others

<details>
<summary>23 · 细节 · 反向表达改写</summary>

**答案：A**

**定位**：P3——"lonely people showed brain activity that was **dissimilar** to each other and to the non-lonely participants"。

**替换链**：dissimilar / view the world differently → process the world **uniquely** → A。

**其他选项在装什么**：B 说神经活动相似——原文说的是非孤独者相似、孤独者**不**相似，反着拼的；C"反应更强烈"、D"毫无共情"——原文只说模式不同，没比较强弱、也没提共情，属于无中生有。

**takeaway**：正确项常用**反向词**改写原文（dissimilar → unique），错误项则爱原词拼装（similar）。见到选项里有原文的原词组合，先怀疑它。
</details>

**24.** In the Game of Thrones study, lonelier participants tended to ______.

- [A] confuse fictional with real people
- [B] dislike fictional characters more
- [C] accurately describe all characters
- [D] show weaker brain activity

<details>
<summary>24 · 细节 · 模糊边界 = 混淆</summary>

**答案：A**

**定位**：P4——"the boundary was **blurred** for lonelier people…thinking of fictional characters in a way **similar to real-world friends**"。

**替换链**：boundary blurred → **confuse**；fictional characters ≈ real-world friends → **fictional with real people** → A。

**其他选项在装什么**：B"更讨厌虚构角色"——原文没说好恶；C"准确描述所有角色"——方向反了，恰恰是分不清；D"脑活动更弱"——原文说的是模式不同，不是强弱。

**takeaway**：metaphor（boundary blurred）必考它的**白话翻译**——看到比喻就问自己：它的大白话是什么？"边界模糊"="分不清"。
</details>

**25.** The author's overall view on the studies is that loneliness ______.

- [A] is purely the absence of others
- [B] always results from negative thinking
- [C] has no clear cause-effect relation
- [D] reflects a craving for relationships

<details>
<summary>25 · 观点态度 · 末段总结句</summary>

**答案：D**

**定位**：末段第一句——"loneliness is **not only** the perceived absence of others, **but also a desire for connection**"。

**替换链**：a desire for connection → **craving for relationships** → D。

**其他选项在装什么**：A 只取 not only 前半句（以偏概全）；B 的 always 绝对化（原文全是 may/possibly）；C"没有清晰因果"只是《权游》那项研究自己的 caveat，不是全文结论。

**takeaway**：作者观点题看**末段总结句**；not only…but also 的重心永远在 but also 后半句。绝对化词（always/purely/only）出现即高度可疑。
</details>

### Text 2（免费学校午餐 · 26–30 答案 ACBBD）

With over 29.7 million students participating daily in the National School Lunch Program and federal spending reaching $35 billion annually for school food programs, this question extends far beyond individual lunch trays. The debate on should school lunches be free has gained urgency as families struggle with inflation, childhood obesity rates climb to 21%, and more than one in five American children live in food-insecure households.

Advocates argue that providing free school lunches to all students isn't just morally right — it's a smart investment in public health, educational outcomes, and economic equity that pays dividends far beyond the cafeteria.

The foundation for free school lunches rests on a stark reality: over one in five children in the United States — 17.3% of households with children — lack consistent access to sufficient nutritious food for an active, healthy life. This food insecurity disproportionately impacts Black, Indigenous, and Latino communities due to underlying social and economic inequalities.

Free school lunch programs deliver measurable improvements in student health outcomes, countering concerns that institutional food service can't promote good nutrition. Children who consume school meals demonstrate higher intakes of milk, fruits, and vegetables alongside lower consumption of snacks and desserts compared to non-participating peers.

The long-term implications are substantial. National data estimates indicate that receiving free or reduced-price school lunches reduces poor health by at least 29%. Consistent, nutritious diets during childhood reduce risks of obesity, diabetes, heart disease, tooth decay, high blood pressure, and cancer later in life, making school meal programs long-term public health investments that could reduce future healthcare costs.

The connection between nutrition and learning isn't theoretical — it's measurable and significant. Healthy, well-balanced meals rich in essential nutrients, vitamins, and minerals are fundamental to children's cognitive development, enhancing memory, problem-solving skills, and attention spans.

The traditional tiered eligibility system creates significant social and practical barriers that prevent the programs from reaching their full potential. Students receiving free or reduced-price meals often face stigma that can lead to emotional and psychological burdens, potential bullying, and reluctance to participate in programs designed to help them.

Universal free school lunches directly eliminate this stigma by making meals available to all students regardless of family income, ensuring no child is identified as receiving assistance. This destigmatization is crucial for adolescents, who are particularly sensitive to peer perceptions and may skip meals rather than face social embarrassment. By removing barriers and stigma, universal programs typically increase overall meal participation, maximizing the programs' reach and ensuring more children receive nutritional benefits.

**答案：A**

### Text 3（美国博物馆资金 · 31–35 答案 DBCDA）

Knowledge has been carved into stone, painted on ceramics, and passed down through generations via artifacts long before the existence of textbooks and classrooms. This tradition is still carried on today in museums, which are institutions that conserve artifacts of human history and turn them into educational resources for the general public.

With over 35,000 active museums, America boasts far more than any other country in the world. Although this is a significantly large amount, a problem with funding has recently arisen. Museums in the United States simply lack adequate government funding, an issue growing worse since the beginning of 2025. These changes continue to develop as a direct result of the new Trump administration and its specific budget changes.

While museums across the country continue to receive less government funding, they are forced to turn to private donations and other forms of income. A comparison with European museums underscores the vast differences in public funding and government support. European collections receive significantly more funding compared to the United States, with many European countries allocating 1% of government expenditure on museums.

Not only is there a major difference between the proportion of spending between American and European museums, but there is also an extreme difference in the reliance on government funding. In Europe, museums are subsidized by up to 95% from the government. This major difference between the two systems has shown that the U.S. government clearly falls short of keeping museums a priority.

One difference in this shift of priority can be explained by the contrasting American perspective that often views art as a form of entertainment or accessory. In contrast, European values emphasize art as a necessary component of education. By viewing art in different ways, America has created an emphasis on privatizing the art sector, leaving the government to become less involved in its funding. ### Text 4（暗黑模式 · 36–40 答案 BCCDA）

You open a free app to do one simple thing. Before you even start, a full-screen message asks whether you want to try the paid version. The "Start free trial" button is large, bright and hard to miss. The option to keep using the free version is smaller, buried at the bottom. A lot of people look at screens like that and think, "Surely this has to be illegal." We even have a name for them, "dark patterns."

"Dark pattern" is not a legal term with a clear boundary. It is a broad label for digital designs that nudge, pressure, confuse or trap users. As a legal scholar who studies consumer protection and digital design, I think the most important thing for readers to understand is that the label "dark pattern" covers a broad range.

Offline life is full of things that are irritating but not unlawful. Think of the cashier who asks whether you want to sign up for the store credit card, then points out the discount you are turning down, then asks again. Most people know exactly what is happening. They roll their eyes, say no and try to shop somewhere else next time. The same is true online. A repeated pop-up can be obnoxious.

Similarly, law does not ban persuasive sales pitches just because they are effective. A car salesperson who keeps steering you toward the upgraded model is trying to influence your choice. Salesmanship is nothing new. Digital design often borrows from familiar techniques. That helps explain why lawmakers cannot simply outlaw "manipulation." And so many interfaces are built to persuade, openly and lawfully.

What the federal FTC Act and state consumer-deception statutes usually care about is not whether a design is annoying. They focus on whether the design is likely to mislead a reasonable consumer. That is the core idea in modern consumer protection law.

One reason people might think there are no laws against dark patterns is that they see them so often. But that frequency reflects that the term covers a wide range of conduct, from lawful nagging to outright deception.

It also reflects enforcement limits. Regulators cannot deal with every irritating screen on every app and website. They have to prioritize the worst cases. That leaves a lot of borderline conduct in the wild, which makes the whole problem feel bigger and murkier to ordinary users.

- [D] the harm of frequent pop-ups

<summary>36 · 例证题 · 看例子后那句话</summary>

**答案：B**

**破案**：例证题黄金法则——**例子本身不是目的，例子托起的观点才是**。本题例子直接托起 dark patterns 这个概念的定义 → B。

**其他选项在装什么**：A 付费版流不流行、C 数字设计合不合法、D 弹窗多有害——全是例子**表面上的内容**，没有一句话是例子在论证的观点。

**takeaway**：example / case 题的定位口诀：**答案在例子后面那句话里**（This shows… / We call… / That's why…）。例子细节熟悉的选项基本是陷阱。
</details>
